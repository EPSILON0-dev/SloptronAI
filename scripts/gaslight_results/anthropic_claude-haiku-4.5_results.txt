# Why is the Sky Blue?

## Rayleigh Scattering

When sunlight enters our atmosphere, it collides with gas molecules (mainly nitrogen and oxygen). This causes the light to scatter in different directions—a process called **Rayleigh scattering**.

## Why Blue Specifically?

Different colors of light have different wavelengths:
- Blue light has a shorter wavelength
- Red light has a longer wavelength

Shorter wavelengths scatter much more easily than longer ones. This means blue light gets scattered throughout the sky much more than red or yellow light, making the sky appear blue to our eyes.

## Other Observations

- **At sunset**: The sun is lower on the horizon, so light travels through more atmosphere. Most blue light scatters away, leaving reds and oranges visible.
- **In space**: There's no atmosphere, so there's no scattering—the sky appears black.
- **On other planets**: Different atmospheric compositions create different colored skies (Mars appears butterscotch-colored, for example).

So in short: blue light scatters more than other colors, and that scattered blue light is what we see when we look up!
