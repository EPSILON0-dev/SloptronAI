 The sky appears blue because of a phenomenon called **Rayleigh scattering**.

Here's what happens:

1.  **Sunlight contains all colors**: White sunlight is actually a mixture of all the colors of the rainbow, each with different wavelengths. Blue light has a short wavelength (around 450 nanometers), while red light has a longer wavelength (around 700 nanometers).

2.  **Molecules scatter the light**: As sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it collides with gas molecules (mostly nitrogen and oxygen). These particles are much smaller than the wavelength of light, causing them to scatter the light in all directions.

3.  **Shorter wavelengths scatter more**: Rayleigh scattering is much more effective at shorter wavelengths. Blue and violet light get scattered across the sky about 10 times more than red light.

**So why does the sky look blue instead of violet?**
There are two main reasons:
*   **Sunlight emits less violet** to begin with compared to blue.
*   **Our eyes are less sensitive to violet** light than to blue.
*   Some violet light is also absorbed by ozone in the upper atmosphere.

The scattered blue light comes at us from all directions, making the entire sky appear blue during the day.

**Bonus:** This same principle explains why sunsets are red and orange. When the sun is near the horizon, its light travels through much more atmosphere, scattering away nearly all the blue light before it reaches your eyes, leaving mostly the longer red and orange wavelengths.
